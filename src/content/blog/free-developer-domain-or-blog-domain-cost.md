## Overview 

Search for a free domain and you will find two kinds of results: honest offers with a catch you can live with, and "$1 domains" that quietly cost twenty times more when the year ends. This post sorts them out, then puts real numbers on the `.blog` extension so you know what you are signing up for before you open a checkout page.

All `.blog` and `.com` prices below come from the tldes price tracker, checked on 14 November 2026: [.blog prices](https://tldes.com/blog) and [.com prices](https://tldes.com/com). The `.com` page showed a 13 November reading when I checked. Registrars change prices without notice, so confirm at checkout.

## "Free" means three different things

| Type | Example | Cost | Catch |
|---|---|---|---|
| Free subdomain | `yourname.github.io`, `yourname.vercel.app` | $0, no expiry | The host's name stays in your URL |
| Free first year | Student Pack domains from Namecheap, .TECH or Name.com | $0 for the offer period | Students only, and renewal is at full price |
| Community subdomain | is-a.dev | $0 | Strict rules, no commercial use |

## Free subdomains: the zero-risk start

If you only need a live link for a portfolio or a test project, a host-provided subdomain is enough. GitHub Pages, Vercel, Netlify and Cloudflare Pages all give you one the moment you deploy. Nothing to renew, nothing to forget.

The cost is ownership. The address belongs to the platform, so moving hosts means a new URL, and every link you collected points at the old one. Fine for practice. A weak foundation for something you plan to grow.

I build and publish from a phone, and a free subdomain is how I would start again from zero. The walkthrough is in [how to build an Astro blog on your phone](https://www.isamueldev.blog/blog/build-astro-blog-on-phone/), and if you have not picked a framework yet, [getting started with Astro](https://www.isamueldev.blog/blog/getting-started-with-astro/) covers the setup.

## The student route: free domain offers

Verified students can claim domains through the [GitHub Student Developer Pack](https://education.github.com/pack), but the three domain offers are not identical:

- **Namecheap:** one year of registration on a `.me` domain, plus a separate free SSL certificate for one year.
- **.TECH:** one standard `.tech` domain, free for one year.
- **Name.com:** a select free domain from more than 25 extensions, including `.app`, `.dev`, `.live` and `.studio`. The pack page does not state the same one-year term for this one, so read Name.com's redemption conditions before you rely on it. Use a unique email address on your Name.com account, or the system may treat your claim as a duplicate.

Two things apply to all three. You must be a verified student, and a free domain is not a free renewal. Before claiming, look up what the same domain costs in year two, and put the renewal date in your calendar.

## is-a.dev: free, but read the terms first

Some developers use is-a.dev for a short, clean address such as `yourname.is-a.dev`, requested through a GitHub pull request. The service is free, and its [terms of service](https://github.com/is-a-dev/register/blob/main/TERMS_OF_SERVICE.md) are strict enough that they matter if you plan to earn from your site:

- **No commercial or for-profit use.** The terms list commercial, for-profit and political purposes as prohibited, and say subdomains may not be monetised, sold or rented.
- **Blogs must be primarily about software development.** A tech and gaming blog, or any general blog, falls outside that.
- **Individuals only.** Registration is for a personal GitHub account, not an organisation.
- **No guarantees.** Maintainers can reject a request for any reason and can terminate a subdomain at any time. The terms were last updated on 31 August 2026, so check the current version.

So is-a.dev suits a personal developer portfolio. It does not suit an ad-supported blog or anything you hope to turn into income.

## What a .blog domain costs

First-year prices for `.blog` are low almost everywhere. Renewals are where the real price shows up.

| Registrar | First year | Renewal per year | 3-year total |
|---|---|---|---|
| Spaceship | $1.88 | $20.18 | $42.24 |
| Porkbun | $1.06 | $21.11 | $43.28 |
| Dynadot | $2.00 | $21.62 | $45.24 |
| Namecheap | $3.18 | $33.18 | $69.54 |
| GoDaddy | $2.19 | $40.19 | $82.57 |

The three-year total is the first year plus two renewals. Some of the lowest first-year prices need a promo code or are limited to one domain, so read the checkout page.

The pattern is plain: the cheapest first year is not always the cheapest domain. Porkbun has the lowest sticker price in this snapshot, but Spaceship comes out slightly cheaper over three years because its renewal is lower. Cloudflare and NicNames also list renewals near $20.20. At the other end, GoDaddy, 123 Reg and NiceNIC all renew above $39 a year.

For comparison, `.com` renewals are much cheaper: the lowest tracked was $10.88 a year, and Namecheap's was $16.18. A `.blog` earns its extra cost when the name reads well as a sentence, like `yourname.blog`.

## Charges that show up at checkout

- **WHOIS privacy.** Many registrars include it free, and some charge for it. Check before you pay.
- **Payment method.** Registrars bill in US dollars. If you pay from Nigeria or anywhere with currency restrictions, confirm your card works for international payments before you start.
- **Auto-renew.** Leave it on, or put the expiry date in your calendar. A lapsed domain is the most expensive mistake on this list.

## Who should pay, and who should stay free

- **Learning or testing:** stay on a free subdomain. A domain adds cost and nothing else.
- **Student building a portfolio:** claim a Student Pack domain, and decide about renewal before the year ends.
- **A blog you plan to keep and earn from:** buy a domain you will own, `.blog` or `.com`, and compare three-year totals. Free community subdomains will not allow monetisation.
- **Not sure yet:** start on a free subdomain and connect a custom domain later. Astro does not care what address it is served from.

One honest note: a domain does nothing for speed. If your pages feel slow, the fix is images and JavaScript, and [these Astro performance tips](https://www.isamueldev.blog/blog/astro-performance-tips/) cover exactly that.

## FAQ

### Is there a completely free .blog domain?

Not that I could find. `.blog` is a paid extension, and even the cheapest registrar charges a first-year fee. If you want $0, use a free subdomain, or a Student Pack domain with a different extension.

### Why is the renewal price so much higher than the first year?

Registrars sell the first year near or below cost to win customers, then charge the standard rate on renewal. It is normal, which is why the three-year total tells you more than the sticker price.

### Can I keep a free student domain after the first year?

Yes, if you pay the renewal. If you do not, it expires and returns to the market. Check the renewal rate before you claim.

### Does a custom domain help my Google ranking?

Not automatically. Google's ranking systems look at many signals, including how relevant and useful your content is, and a domain name is not a shortcut. A memorable domain makes your site easier to recognise and remember, and it lets you change hosts without losing your address.

### Can I move my domain to another registrar later?

Usually, but eligibility depends on the [ICANN transfer policy](https://www.icann.org/en/contracted-parties/accredited-registrars/resources/domain-name-transfers/policy) and your registrar's own rules. A registrar may refuse a transfer within 60 days of a domain's creation or of an earlier transfer, and a change of registrant details can trigger another 60-day lock unless you opted out. Transfers also often cost about one year of renewal.

### Should I choose .blog or .com?

If `yourname.com` is available and affordable, it is the safer default and the cheaper one to renew. Pick `.blog` when the name works better with it or the `.com` is taken.
